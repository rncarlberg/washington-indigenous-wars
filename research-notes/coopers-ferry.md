# Cooper’s Ferry / Nipéhe research notes

Last reviewed: October 23, 2026.

Editorial working notes for Washington Indigenous Wars. Preliminary material below is not part of the website’s historical narrative.

## Preliminary canid research — watch for follow-up

Paulson, Kirsten. “A Terminal Pleistocene Puppy? Virtual Analysis of a Juvenile Canid from Cooper’s Ferry, Idaho.” Paleoamerican Odyssey conference, October 2026, poster PB-58.

Source: https://liberalarts.tamu.edu/csfa/paleoamerican-odyssey-conference-program/
Find “Terminal Pleistocene Puppy” in the program and abstracts.

The abstract describes a juvenile canid cranium and mandible from Feature 110. Earlier jaw comparisons suggested a coyote or possible coyote–domestic dog hybrid. CT scanning allowed examination of the fragile skull within its sediment block; newer cranial measurements are unusually small compared with wolves and coyotes. The abstract interprets these measurements as more consistent with a juvenile domestic dog and gives an approximate age of 11,300 calibrated years before present.

**Uncertainty:** The abstract also states that species identification remains uncertain without ancient DNA. It does not explain the dating basis or demonstrate a domestic dog at the site 16,000 years ago. A conference abstract is preliminary evidence.

**Editorial decision:** Keep this in research notes; do not add a domestic-dog claim to the timeline. Reconsider when a full publication provides dating, archaeological context, and stronger identification evidence.
